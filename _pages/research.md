My research integrates geotechnical engineering, stochastic modelling and probabilistic risk assessment to understand how climate change and interacting natural hazards affect slopes and transport infrastructure.

## 1. Climate Change and Rainfall Hazards

I investigate how changing rainfall characteristics influence natural hazard risk, with particular emphasis on non-stationary stochastic rainfall modelling, extreme rainfall events and the temporal and spatial dependence of rainfall.

**Research topics:** Non-stationary rainfall modelling; rainfall dependence; spatially extensive extreme rainfall; climate projections.

**Representative publication:** [Probabilistic assessment of shallow landslides in a changing climate: Insight from the rainfall dependence structure](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10346-026-02773-2).

## 2. Landslide Mechanics and Risk

I study rainfall infiltration and slope failure using physically based and probabilistic approaches. My research also explores how vegetation modifies soil hydraulic and mechanical properties and how these changes influence slope stability.

**Research topics:** Rainfall-induced landslides; transient infiltration; slope fragility; vegetation–soil–water interactions.

## 3. Multi-Hazard Risk Assessment

I develop probabilistic approaches to assess slope performance under multiple natural hazards, particularly rainfall and earthquakes, accounting for changing hazard occurrence and uncertainty over the service life of infrastructure.

**Research topics:** Rainfall–earthquake interactions; life-cycle reliability; multi-hazard fragility; probabilistic hazard modelling.

## 4. Infrastructure Risk and Resilience

I investigate how spatially distributed natural hazards affect transport infrastructure and network performance, linking component-level failures to network connectivity and resilience.

**Research topics:** Spatially correlated landslides; road network connectivity; infrastructure fragility; recovery and resilience strategies.
